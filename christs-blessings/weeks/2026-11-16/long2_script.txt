## A mind that won't slow down | storm
It's late, the house is quiet, and your body is tired. But your mind is still running. One thought leads to another, and another, like rain that keeps tapping on the window.
If that's where you are tonight, you're not doing anything wrong. Many faithful people have lain awake with racing thoughts, including the people who wrote the Psalms. [pause]
This is a sleep prayer for anxious thoughts. We'll listen to a few short lines of scripture, breathe slowly, and let the body grow heavy. You don't have to make the thoughts stop. We'll simply bring them to God, one at a time, and let Him hold them. [pause]
If you fall asleep partway through, that's alright. After the prayer, quiet music will stay with you for a long while. [pause]
Let the sound of the rain be the only thing you need to listen to for a moment. [pause]
Breathe in through your nose, slowly. [pause]
Hold it for just a moment at the top. [pause]
And let it out through your mouth, longer and slower than it came in. [long pause]
## The multitude of my thoughts | storm
There's a verse in Psalm 94 that sounds as if it were written at midnight.
"In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul." [pause]
The multitude of my thoughts. A crowd. That's what an anxious mind can feel like, a room full of people all talking at once. [pause]
But notice what the psalmist doesn't say. He doesn't say, when my thoughts finally went quiet, then God comforted me. He says, in the multitude. Right in the middle of the crowd, God's comforts found him. [long pause]
So you don't need an empty mind to meet God tonight. He can comfort you in the noise. [long pause]
Let's try something gentle. When a thought comes, don't argue with it, and don't chase it. Just notice it, and give it a simple name. That's work. [pause] That's money. [pause] That's family. [pause] That's tomorrow. [pause]
Then let it walk to the edge of the room, like someone stepping out to make a phone call. [long pause]
Lord, here is the crowd in my head. Comfort me right in the middle of it. [long pause]
## Setting the heavy bag down | storm
Psalm 55 gives us a picture to hold.
"Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee:" [pause]
Imagine you've been carrying a heavy bag all day. The strap is digging into your shoulder. You've carried it so long you've almost stopped noticing the weight. [pause]
Now picture yourself at the end of a long road, at the door of a warm house. Someone kind opens the door and says, Let me take that. [pause]
Feel the strap slide off your shoulder. Feel the weight leave your arm. [long pause]
Let your shoulders fall. Let your neck lengthen on the pillow. Let your hands go loose. [long pause]
Casting a burden isn't pretending it doesn't matter. It's letting someone stronger carry it for the night. And the promise is that He will sustain you. Hold you up. Keep you going. [long pause]
## What time I am afraid | galilee_dusk
The rain is easing now.
Psalm 56 has one of the shortest prayers in the whole Bible. David wrote it at a frightening time in his life.
"What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee." [pause]
He doesn't say, I am never afraid. He says, what time I am afraid. Fear came for David too. And when it did, he had a sentence ready. [pause]
Let's make that sentence our breath prayer tonight. [pause]
Breathing in: What time I am afraid... [pause]
Breathing out: I will trust in thee. [pause]
Breathing in: What time I am afraid... [pause]
Breathing out: I will trust in thee. [long pause]
Keep that rhythm on your own for a few breaths. If a worry interrupts, that's alright. Just begin the sentence again. [long pause]
## In quietness and confidence | galilee_dusk
The prophet Isaiah once gave a tired, worried people a word from God. Part of it says:
"In returning and rest shall ye be saved; in quietness and in confidence shall be your strength:" [pause]
Returning and rest. Quietness and confidence. Strength doesn't always look like doing more. Sometimes strength looks like lying still. [pause]
So let's rest, one part of the body at a time. [pause]
Start with your feet. Let them grow warm and heavy, as if they're sinking into the bed. [long pause]
Now your legs. Let the muscles in your calves and thighs let go. [long pause]
Now your back and your hips. Let the mattress take all of your weight. You don't need to hold any of it up. [long pause]
Now your chest. Let it rise and fall on its own, without any effort from you. [long pause]
Now your hands and arms, heavy and still. [pause]
Your jaw, loose. Your eyes, soft behind their lids. Your forehead, smooth. [long pause]
In quietness and in confidence. This is strength too. [long pause]
## He is at my right hand | night_lake
One more psalm, from David again. Psalm 16.
"I have set the LORD always before me: because he is at my right hand, I shall not be moved. Therefore my heart is glad, and my glory rejoiceth: my flesh also shall rest in hope." [pause]
I have set the LORD before me. Anxiety often sets tomorrow before us, and every way it could go wrong. David chose to set something else in front of his eyes. [pause]
Picture the Lord, not far off, but right beside you, at your right hand, the way a friend sits by a bed in the night. Steady. Unhurried. Staying. [long pause]
And then the lovely last line: my flesh also shall rest in hope. Even your body can rest now, not because every question is answered, but because you are not alone with them. [long pause]
Lord, You are at my right hand. Let my body rest in hope tonight. [long pause]
## For the days after tonight | night_lake
Before we finish, one gentle word. [pause]
If anxious thoughts are with you most nights, or most days, please don't carry that alone. Talking with a doctor, a counselor, or someone you trust is a good and wise step, and it can go hand in hand with prayer. Asking for help is not a lack of faith. [long pause]

The Lord of peace himself. Not a technique, not a trick for sleeping. Himself. [pause]
