## Before you close your eyes | candle
Some nights it isn't our own life that keeps us awake. It's theirs. A son who hasn't called. A daughter far from home. A parent who is getting older. A partner carrying something heavy. A grandchild you pray for every day.
If that's why you're here tonight, you're in the right place. This is a sleep prayer for the people you love. We're going to bring them to God one by one, leave them in His care, and then let you rest. [pause]
There's nothing you need to do well here. Just listen. If you fall asleep partway through, that's alright. The prayer will keep going, and so will His love for them. [pause]
Settle into your bed. Let your head sink into the pillow. [pause]
Take a slow breath in... and let it out through your mouth, like a long sigh. [pause]
Once more. Breathe in... and let the day go. [long pause]
## The God who is near | candle
Before we pray for anyone else, let's remember who we're talking to.
Psalm 145, verse 18, says: "The LORD is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth." [pause]
Nigh means near. Close enough to hear a whisper. You don't need a long prayer or perfect words tonight. Calling on Him in truth simply means being honest. [pause]
So let's be honest. Lord, I love these people, and I worry about them. Sometimes I worry more than I pray. Tonight I want to give them to You. [long pause]
## The Shepherd and the lambs | galilee_dusk
The prophet Isaiah gave us a picture of how God cares for His people. Isaiah 40, verse 11:
"He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom, and shall gently lead those that are with young." [pause]
A shepherd counts his flock every evening. He knows which one limps, which one wanders, which one is too small to keep up. And the little ones, he carries close to his chest. [pause]
Notice the last line too: he gently leads those that are with young. The ones who are caring for others. That may be you tonight, the parent, the carer, the one who looks after everyone. He leads you gently too. [long pause]
## Naming them one by one | galilee_dusk
Now, let's bring each person into the Shepherd's arms. I'll pause after each one, so you can picture their face and say their name in your heart.
First, someone in your home tonight, or someone who used to be. [long pause]
Lord, carry them close. Give them rest, and let them know they are loved. [pause]
Now, someone who lives far away from you. Maybe in another city, or another country. [long pause]
Lord, You are as near to them as You are to me. Be their Shepherd tonight. [pause]
Now, someone whose path worries you. Someone who seems to be wandering, or struggling, or shutting you out. [long pause]
Lord, You know where they are, even when I don't. Keep looking after them. [pause]
Now, someone who is unwell, or getting older, or very tired. [long pause]
Lord, hold them gently. Give them comfort, and good people around them. [pause]
And now, anyone else who comes to mind. Let their faces come and go, without hurrying. [long pause]
Thank You, Lord, for every one of them. [long pause]
## What Jesus prayed for His own | candle
On the night before the cross, Jesus prayed for His disciples, the people He loved most on earth. One line from that prayer, in John 17, verse 15, is a prayer you can borrow for your family tonight:
"I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil." [pause]
Jesus didn't ask for His friends to have easy lives, or to be hidden away from every hard thing. He asked that they would be kept. [pause]
We can't keep the people we love from everything. We can't follow them to work, or into their thoughts, or through every decision. But we can ask the One who goes everywhere to keep them. [pause]
So pray it with me, slowly. Father, I don't ask You to take them out of the world. I ask You to keep them. [long pause]
Keep their minds. [pause] Keep their hearts. [pause] Keep their steps. [long pause]
## Letting go of the worrying | candle
Now there's something to put down.
Many of us carry our family's problems to bed as if lying awake might fix them. But worry can't drive a car more safely, or heal a body, or change a heart. Only love can do some things, and only God can do others. [pause]
Psalm 103, verse 13, says: "Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him." [pause]
To pity here means to feel deep compassion, the kind a good parent feels for a child who is hurting. Whatever tenderness you feel for your family tonight, God feels for them too. And He feels it for you. [long pause]
So picture yourself setting your worries down, one at a time, on a table beside the bed. [pause]
The worry about their safety. Set it down. [long pause]
The worry about their choices. Set it down. [long pause]
The worry about the future. Set it down. [long pause]
They're still loved. They're still prayed for. You're just not carrying them alone through the night anymore. [long pause]
## A blessing for your household | night_lake
Let's close with a blessing that Paul wrote to a church he loved, in 2 Thessalonians 3, verse 16:
"Now the Lord of peace himself give you peace always by all means. The Lord be with you all." [pause]
Be with you all. Not just the ones who are doing well. All of them. [pause]
Receive it now, for yourself and for your family.
May the Lord of peace give peace to every person you love tonight. [pause]
May He carry the little ones and gently lead the ones who care for them. [pause]
May He keep them in this world, and keep them from the evil. [pause]
And may you sleep tonight knowing that every name you spoke is held in His hands. In Jesus' name, Amen. [long pause]
You've done what love can do tonight. You've prayed. Now rest. Goodnight.
