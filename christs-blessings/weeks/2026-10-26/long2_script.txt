## When the ground shifts | calm_dawn
Psalm 46 was written for a time when everything felt unsteady. It doesn't begin with calm. It begins with God, in the middle of upheaval.
Listen to the first three verses.
"God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea; Though the waters thereof roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof. Selah." [pause]
Mountains were the most solid things people knew. The psalm imagines even those sliding into the sea. Maybe something in your life that felt as solid as a mountain has moved lately. A job. Your health. A relationship. A plan you were sure of. [pause]
And notice what the psalm doesn't say. It doesn't say the earth will never shake. It says, "therefore will not we fear." The reason isn't that the ground stays still. The reason is who stands with us on it. [long pause]
"A very present help." Not a distant help. Not a help that arrives once things settle down. Present. Here. Now, in this minute, as you listen. [pause]
Let's sit with that phrase for a moment. A very present help in trouble. [long pause]
The word "Selah" appears at the end of verse 3. No one is completely sure what it meant, but many believe it marked a pause in the music, a place to stop and let the words sink in. So let's do exactly that. [long pause]
## The river that keeps flowing | candle
Then the psalm changes its picture. After the roaring sea, we hear about a gentle river.
"There is a river, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the most High. God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved: God shall help her, and that right early." [pause]
Jerusalem had no great river running through it. Water had to be carried, stored, and protected. So this river is a promise of something deeper: a supply of gladness that doesn't depend on what is happening outside the walls. [pause]
Is there a dry place in you tonight? A place where joy has felt far away for a while? Imagine that river finding its way there. Slowly. Quietly. Without forcing anything. [long pause]
"God is in the midst of her." In the middle. Not watching from the edge. [pause]
"She shall not be moved." Things around you may move. The centre doesn't have to. [pause]
"God shall help her, and that right early." Right early means at the turning of the morning, when the night is ending. If this is a long night for you, it is not the end of the story. Morning comes. [long pause]
## Holding your peace | candle
Long before this psalm was written, the people of Israel stood at the edge of the Red Sea with an army behind them and water in front. They were terrified, and Moses told them this, in Exodus 14, verse 14:
"The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace." [pause]
Hold your peace. Not because nothing matters, but because the outcome was not theirs to carry. Some battles are yours to fight. Others you are invited to set down. [pause]
Ask yourself gently: is there something I've been fighting in my own strength that I can hand over tonight? [long pause]
Lord, here is what I've been fighting. I hand it to You. Help me hold my peace. [long pause]
## Be still, and know | calm_dawn
Now we come to the most well-known line of this psalm, verse 10.
"Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth." [pause]
In the middle of a song about earthquakes and roaring seas, God speaks directly. And the first thing He says isn't "try harder" or "fix it". It's "be still". Stop striving. Let your hands drop. [pause]
Then, "and know". Stillness here isn't emptiness. It's making room to know something: that He is God, and you don't have to be. [long pause]
We'll pray this line now, a little shorter each time, letting each word settle. Just listen, or say it quietly with me.
Be still, and know that I am God. [long pause]
Be still, and know. [long pause]
Be still. [long pause]
Be. [long pause]
Rest there for a moment. You are allowed simply to be, in the presence of the One who made you. [long pause]
## The God of Jacob | sunrise_lake
The psalm ends with these words, verse 11: "The LORD of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge. Selah." [pause]
The Lord of hosts, the God of all the armies of heaven. And in the same breath, the God of Jacob. Jacob was not a hero who had everything together. He was restless, he made mistakes, and he spent years running. Yet God called Himself by Jacob's name. [pause]
That means the God of this psalm isn't only the God of strong people. He's the God of the ones who have struggled and stumbled. He's your refuge too. [long pause]
## A prayer of stillness | sunrise_lake
Let's pray.
Lord, You are my refuge and my strength, a very present help in trouble. When the ground shifts under me, be the centre that does not move. Let Your river reach the dry places in my heart. Teach me to hold my peace and let You carry what I can't. And in the noise of this week, remind me again to be still, and know that You are God. In Jesus' name, Amen. [long pause]
