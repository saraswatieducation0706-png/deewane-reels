## A song for the road | night_lake
Psalm 121 is one of the Songs of Degrees, sometimes called the songs of ascent. Travellers sang them as they walked up the hills towards Jerusalem for the great feasts. The road was long, the nights were spent outdoors, and there were no walls around them. These were songs for people who felt exposed.
So as we walk through it now, let it be your song for the night. [pause]
"I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help." [pause]
Picture the traveller looking up at the dark hills ahead. They could hide danger, or they could hide nothing at all. Either way, the question is honest: where does my help come from? [long pause]
"My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth." [pause]
Not from the hills. From the One who made them. The same hands that set the stars above your roof tonight are the hands holding your life. [long pause]
"He will not suffer thy foot to be moved: he that keepeth thee will not slumber." [pause]
On a rocky path, one slip in the dark could hurt you. This verse says He watches your footing, even the steps you'll take tomorrow that you can't see yet. [long pause]
"Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep." [pause]
Here is the heart of it. Someone has to stay awake tonight, and it doesn't have to be you. You can close your eyes because His are open. [long pause]
"The LORD is thy keeper: the LORD is thy shade upon thy right hand." [pause]
A keeper is someone who stays close and pays attention. Shade is shelter from what would wear you down. He is near enough to cover you. [long pause]
"The sun shall not smite thee by day, nor the moon by night." [pause]
Day and night. The pressures of the daytime and the fears that come out after dark. Both are covered. [long pause]
"The LORD shall preserve thee from all evil: he shall preserve thy soul." [pause]
Your soul, the deepest part of you, the part no one else can see, is in His keeping. [long pause]
"The LORD shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in from this time forth, and even for evermore." [pause]
Your going out this morning, and your coming in tonight. Every door you walked through, He walked through first. [long pause]
## Leaving tomorrow at the door | night_lake
Sometimes it isn't today that keeps us awake. It's tomorrow. The meeting, the appointment, the conversation we're dreading, the bill that's due.
Jesus spoke about this in Matthew 6, verse 34: "Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof."
In other words, tomorrow will have enough of its own. You don't have to live it tonight. [pause]
Picture tomorrow as a closed door at the end of a hallway. You'll open it in the morning, and God will already be on the other side. For now, you can leave it closed. [pause]
Lord, I leave tomorrow with You. I don't need to rehearse it, solve it, or worry it into shape. Meet me there when morning comes. [long pause]
